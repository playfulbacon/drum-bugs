DrumBugs

·

Teach a bug a rhythm. It loops that rhythm forever.
Its routine is its drum part — so building the workforce is writing the song.

  • Berries buy bugs. Click one in the tray and it crawls out of the lit nest, straight into teaching. Click another nest to make it the one they come from.
  • Drum the routine with W A S D — north, west, south, east — and space to act. Recording starts the moment you pick the bug up. The recorder is the world's grid — slot 1 of a routine is the world's downbeat — so every routine stays locked to every other. The loop goes round while you build it; nothing commits until you confirm, and the bug then comes in at the top of its next lap.
  • Aim by walking into things. A move always turns the bug, even when the way is blocked — so nose into a rock, then act.
  • Aim for a loop that closes — one that comes back to where it started. close loop fills the trailing rests with the walk home. A routine that travels instead is a bug going somewhere; re-teach it when it arrives.
  • Berry bushes regrow, so a good circuit keeps earning. A bug that achieves nothing for a whole lap goes quiet until something changes.
  • Then conduct. 1–4 cue sections in and out, QWER nudge them a beat late, ASDF accent their next action. On the beat it lands now; off the beat it waits for the bar.
  • Ants carry berries to any nest. Beetles smash rock. Crickets jump water and kick the berry they're standing on. Termites raise a new nest where they stand — one time, and it spends them.
  • Grow outward. Collect berries, break the wall, plant a nest past it, teach new circuits from there. Three nests wins.
